Types of Slots in the Lobby

Whatever the name and theme, slots fall into a few core mechanics; knowing the type tells you in advance how a game will behave.

Classic and Payline Slots

Three to five reels, fixed paylines and symbols that match from left to right. The simplest type to learn; the paytable lists the value of every combination.

Cluster Pays and Tumble Slots

Instead of paylines, a grid such as 7x7 where five or more identical symbols touching each other pay. Winning symbols disappear, new ones drop in and a chain can form within a single spin. The example of this type at NK101 is Sugar Rush 1000, where multiplier spots climb to x1,024.

Megaways and Expanding Reels

The height of each reel changes on every spin, so the number of ways to win runs from hundreds to hundreds of thousands. Volatility is usually high.

Jackpot and Hold-and-Win Slots

Fixed or growing jackpots, usually reached through a respin feature that collects coin symbols. Many titles from the Asian studios follow this format.

Bonus Buy Slots

Games where a fixed multiple of the bet buys direct entry to the free spins. It saves time but costs more per feature; the option is available only where the button is shown inside the game.

RTP and Volatility Explained

RTP (Return to Player) is the theoretical percentage a game returns over the long run: a slot with 96.5% RTP returns, over millions of spins, an average of 96.5 from every 100 wagered. A single session can land far above or below that. Volatility describes the pattern of wins: small and frequent, or large and rare.

Bet Sizes in NPR

Bets are shown in rupees in the game's bet menu. The minimum and maximum differ between titles and providers, so check the menu before spinning. A practical rule: choose a bet that lets the session budget cover at least 100-200 spins, and more on a high-volatility game, where a long dry spell before a feature is normal.
